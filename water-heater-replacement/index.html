Symptoms That Mean It's Time to Call

Catching a symptom early is often the difference between a quick part swap and a tank that gives out on the garage floor. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Higher gas or electric bills for less hot water
  • Water leaking from the bottom or body of the tank
  • Rust flaking from the tank jacket or its connections
  • A water heater that is more than about ten years old
  • A household that has outgrown the tank's capacity
  • Loud rumbling or popping that flushing no longer fixes
  • Discolored hot water, a sign the tank is corroding inside
  • A second or third repair within a year or two

Why This Job Calls for a Pro

Start with the venting. A gas water heater's exhaust contains carbon monoxide, and a vent that is undersized, poorly pitched, or loosely connected can let it spill back into your home. Add a gas connection that has to be leak-free, an electrical circuit that has to match the new heater's load, and a relief valve that has to carry the right rating with a discharge line that is never capped or undersized, and there is a lot to get right. Many homes also need an expansion tank, a drain pan, or seismic straps under local code, and some jurisdictions require a permit. A move to tankless or a heat pump brings gas line sizing, new vent materials, circuits, and condensate lines into the job.

Professional service handles the full job, not just the tank. Sizing is done by first-hour rating or flow rate. Fuel, venting, and electrical capacity are verified, and whatever expansion tank, drain pan, or straps your home needs get added. We leak-test every joint, check draft and carbon monoxide on gas units, and haul the old heater off. Simple Ways to Extend Your Water Heater's Life

A few simple habits, like an annual flush and a quick look at the relief valve, can add years to a tank. Here are our recommendations:

  • Flush the new tank once or twice a year to keep sediment from building up
  • A fresh anode rod at the right time is the cheapest way to extend tank life
  • Plan ahead: knowing the heater's age lets you replace it on your schedule, not after a leak
  • Have water pressure checked; above about 80 psi, a pressure reducing valve protects the new heater
  • Set the thermostat around 120 degrees to prevent scalding and reduce wear
  • Descale a tankless unit every year, or more often with hard water
  • Clean the air filter on a heat pump water heater every few months
